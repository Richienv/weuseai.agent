# Physical Realism and Continuity

Read this when people, rooms, doors, props, scale, or a recurring location appear.

## Continuity hierarchy

Protect in this order. Repeat invariant phrases exactly. Do not restyle them.

1. Face and body identity (same person as the still; age, height, hair, clothes).
2. Hero-object geometry (one door, two faces; one lock; one bouquet).
3. Wardrobe and body state (boots on, dry vs wet, clean chin, no extra sweat).
4. Room architecture and object placement (the master still is law).
5. Light, weather, hour, palette.
6. Background action — usually there is none.

Carry a state change forward after the beat that causes it. A closed door stays
closed. A wet man stays wet. A recognition face stays blank until the take ends.

## The room is a still that can move

When a master interior exists, list the objects that must remain:

positions, materials, practicals. `Do not add, delete, or move.`

Typical failure: the model "directs" the room — extra shoes, a second lamp,
a rearranged cabinet, a mirrored wall. Kill that in REFERENCES and LOCKS.

Still-life props that look usable are still-life until the timeline says
otherwise (`the slippers are objects; she never touches them`).

Period lock: if the world is mid-1990s Hong Kong, nothing after that decade
enters the frame. Do not write "vintage" — name the objects that already exist.

## Two-sided objects

Doors, cards, coats, signs, painted panels: the model loves a second copy
or it paints the graphic on the wrong face.

Write all four:

1. `One [object], two faces.`
2. Outside face = [graphic / material] from <<<A>>>.
3. Inside face = [graphic / material] from <<<B>>>.
4. Swing + hinge relative to this camera, and what is visible mid-move.

Then the never-rules: no second object, no graphic on the wrong face, no
handle teleport, no hinge swap, no mirror.

If the camera is inside and the door opens in, the outside face can flash
toward lens on the open slab. If the door opens out, the outside face can
stand in the doorway, angled back at an interior camera. Pick one and keep it
for the whole series.

A slam-to-black is the inside face covering the lens, not a dissolve.

## Scale needs a ruler

The model shrinks men and raises railings. Give a real-world ruler and a
body relationship:

- `Rail ~110 cm. Man 185 cm. When he stands straight against it, the top rail
  sits just above his belt, at the lower ribs — well below the chest. Most of
  him is above the rail. His shoulders are wider than the gap between two posts.`
- `She is 158 cm, slight. After she enters you see the crown and shoulders
  toward a high camera; you can still read her cheek. Not a vertical top-shot.`
- `Camera = her eye at 148 cm. He reads tall. Lens looks slightly up at him.`

Ban the failure in the same breath: `never a rail at chest height, never a
shorter man, never a rail that changes height during the take.`

## Light is physics

| Allowed | Forbidden unless asked |
|---|---|
| One practical, constant color and output | Lamp flicker, swell, cut, color shift |
| Second color entering through a real opening | Motivated "cinematic" key from nowhere |
| Shape on floor/face changing as a door/body occludes | Fade, wash, exposure breathe |
| Body walking from a dark zone into a lit zone | Lights turning on as acting |
| End black because a slab covers the lens | End black as a grade |

Name brightness layers when inside and outside share a frame:

1. Darkest = interior (a low red lamp, everything else in the well).
2. Mid = the slab / jamb, half in, half out.
3. Brightest = the night outside.

`The interior is never bright, never glowing, never a field of red.`

On a face in close-up: one rake (red from the practical and the beads), the
other side held in cooler shadow. `Never flatten. Never lift the shadows.`

## Camera is a machine, not a style

Default: bolted. Breath is a human operator trying to hold still, not a
handheld aesthetic.

Write numbers:

- Height: `2.3 m`, `148 cm (her eye)`, `on the lock`.
- Angle: `~40° down`, `slightly up at him`, `level on the lock`.
- Place: `inner hall, high corner facing the door`, `in the bead doorway
  looking into the kitchen`, `tight on the lock body`.
- Frame: `door, mat, slippers, a sliver of the low cabinet, the tiles`;
  `only two hands and the lock`; `only her face, off-center`.

Focus is a decision, restated in LOCKS:

- `Focus nailed to the doorway and to her.`
- `Focus nailed to the keyway and the tool tips; door panel heavy blur.`
- `Focus nailed to her face for the whole take; beads stay foreground smear;
  the window stays background smear. No rack.`

Lens language that is allowed because it is mechanical:

- `50mm anamorphic, wide open, vertical oval bokeh, halation on the lamp`
- `300mm anamorphic telephoto, paper-thin depth, background crushed`

Lens language that is not allowed: `cinematic camera movement`, `dynamic
framing`, `keep the camera alive`.

Never push, pull, zoom, or travel unless the user asked. A POV shot is still
almost still — her eye, not a swaying helmet cam.

## People

Use only what is visible or requested.

- Age, height, build, hair, exact clothes, bag, shoes. `Boots stay on.`
- Face: same **skull** as the identity stills, written as geometry (width,
  eye size, bridge, mouth, jaw), not "handsome" or "same person as the still."
  For Richie, paste the FACE LOCK from `identity-richie.md`. For Renita,
  paste `identity-renita.md`. Hero close-up wins hair. 6-angle sheet wins
  head turn. Scene screenshots do not donate a face. Renita is 153 cm;
  never stretch her.
- Pores, grain, no plastic skin — as material, not as a slogan.
- State is law: `clean, dry chin, no droplets, no sweat, hair not wet` **or**
  `wet hair stuck to the forehead, water sheen, dark wet patches on the
  shoulders` — pick the one the story needs and do not mix them.
- Ears and hairline: if the still is clean at the ears, say `sideburns and
  ears clean; only slightly wet wisps at the ear, no extra hair`.
- Body weight in contact: heels on tile, waist finding a rail, fingers
  loading a pick, a breath after a strike.
- Gaze: at a real target (the beads, the man, the lock), never the lens
  unless the shot is her POV looking at him.

Do not write `Face stable throughout, no deformation.` Write the actual
face rules: lips closed and still, no bite, no press, no speech, no smile,
no tears, no nose wrinkle, accent = 1 mm of eyelid.

One person in frame unless the user staged two. Never split one protagonist
into two bodies. Never promote a still-life extra into a second actor.

## Performance amplitude

These models overact. Write the size.

- Daily automatic: open, enter, shut, no thought. The only acting is a
  half-beat of easing when the latch catches.
- Practiced hands: muscle memory, a little hurry, never clumsy, never a
  modern pick gun.
- Recognition: at a named second, eyes open a fraction, brow releases,
  the hardness leaves. Then she holds the blank. Nothing else moves.
- Offense: time to play two beats — true daze (look down at the flowers),
  then heat climbing into the eyes. Not an instant switch. Not sad. Not pleading.

If the take is a face, the mouth is usually a lock: closed, soft, dead still.

## Props and acquisition

- Lock silhouette, material, wear, and where it sits on the object.
- Two tools mean two different tools, one per hand, 100% as the still.
- A bouquet that hits a chest is the same bouquet after impact (broken stem
  still broken).
- Nothing teleports into a hand. If they are already working, the tools are
  already in the keyway at 0.0s.

## Audio

- Sound is caused by the beat that makes it.
- Default: mechanics + room + night. No score.
- A diegetic song is a physical source in the room (boombox, off to the side).
- Dialogue only when asked; then it is timed and the mouth is allowed to move.
- No subtitles.

## Series continuity

When shots share an apartment or a pair of people, reuse the same nouns,
hexes, heights, and door law. Do not rename the tiger door. Do not flip the
hinge between shots. Do not restyle the red lamp.

`Never mirror-flip` belongs in LOCKS for any still-matched room.

## Failure repairs

| Failure | First correction |
|---|---|
| Second door / graphic on the wrong face | Restate one object, two faces, hinge, mid-swing visibility |
| Room "improved" | Relist the master still; no add/delete/move |
| Lamp show | One source, constant; change = occlusion or travel through a zone |
| Floaty / overacted | Automatic motion + one named micro-accent; ban the extras |
| Identity drift | Paste the FACE LOCK. Attach hero then 6-angle sheet. Mark scene stills composition-only. If it still copies the actor, drop the scene still |
| Waxy skin | Pores, grain, motivated rake, no plastic — as material |
| Tiny man / giant rail | Put the ruler back in PHYSICS and LOCKS |
| Camera starts directing | Fixed machine, numbers, no push/zoom/orbit |
| Slow-mo / ramp appeared | Real time unless the user wrote a ramp |
| Extra person | `Only X is in frame` |
| Prompt too long | Compress repeated prose; never split parts |
| Too AI | Delete adjectives; keep objects, numbers, clock, never-rules |

## Phrases that are allowed because they are physical

`one continuous shot, no cut` · `real time` · `camera does not move` ·
`handheld breath only` · `this is occlusion, not a lamp change` ·
`one door, two faces` · `100% as <<<handle>>>` · `never add, delete, or move` ·
`focus nailed to …` · `only X is in frame` · `the accent is 1 mm` ·
`already happened, off-screen, before frame 1` · `no 1990s-and-later objects` ·
`never mirror-flip`
