# Why it travels — levers, distribution, retention, series

Read this to diagnose *why* a concept will or won't move, to choose the action you're
designing for, or to build a series. Packaging gets the click; these decide whether
the algorithm keeps pushing it.

## The travel levers (what makes people engage)

A concept needs at least two of these firing. Name them explicitly for each idea:

- **Curiosity gap** — an open loop the viewer needs closed. The engine of watch-time.
- **Specificity** — a concrete number/detail that reads as true and screenshot-able.
- **Emotion** — awe, outrage, recognition, fear, delight. Emotion drives shares.
- **Identity / relatability** — "this is so me" / "this is my people." Drives shares
  and follows.
- **Social currency** — sharing it makes the *sharer* look smart, early, or in-the-know.
- **Utility** — a save-able tool/tip. Drives saves and returns.
- **Transgression / mild taboo** — saying the slightly-not-allowed thing. Drives comments
  and watch-time, but keep it honest and on-brand, not cheap.
- **Debate** — a defensible take people argue with. Drives comments (and comments drive
  reach).

## Design for ONE action: share, save, or comment

Vaguely "good" content underperforms content engineered for a specific action. Decide
the action *before* writing, and shape the ending and caption to trigger it:

- **Share** ← emotion, identity, social currency. Ending: a line worth sending to a
  friend. ("send this to someone who…")
- **Save** ← utility, density, completeness. Ending: "save this for when you…"
- **Comment** ← debate, a question, mild transgression, a fill-in-the-blank. Ending:
  ask the actual question; take a side worth arguing.
- **Follow** ← series promise + a person worth following. Ending: the next-episode hook,
  never "follow for more" (which signals the video is over and tanks watch-time).

Picking three actions at once = picking none. Choose one primary.

## Retention — surviving the middle

New/small accounts bleed viewers at 8–20 seconds. The package wins the click; retention
wins the algorithm's next push.
- **Open loop early** — pose the question the payoff answers, in the first 2s.
- **Re-hook at ~50%** — a second curiosity spike or a "but here's the part nobody
  expects" pivot so mid-video drop-off doesn't kill it.
- **Bookend** — the ending closes the exact loop the hook opened. Promise kept = the
  watch felt worth it = shares/saves.
- **Cut filler, not value.** Remove throat-clearing and repetition; never cut the story,
  proof, or reframe that makes it land. Density is the goal; control runtime with edit
  speed (~1.3–1.5x), not by gutting substance.

## Series / binge design (the cheapest retention multiplier)

A named series turns one-off viewers into a returning audience and a binge ladder.
- **Name it.** "Lean Logic," "Road to 100k," "365." A name signals there's more.
- **Number the episodes** so viewers feel a sequence and can binge.
- **Each episode needs its OWN hook on the cover.** "Day 4" is not a hook; "Day 4: I let
  my agent spend my money" is. The series name + episode hook stack.
- **End on the next loop.** Tease what tomorrow's episode answers.
- **Vary the format across episodes** so the series doesn't feel like one note repeated.

## The honesty gate (why it's also good strategy, not just ethics)

Never fabricate a number, result, credential, or follower count. Beyond being the right
thing, it's *better packaging* for a small account: discovery/process framing ("here's
what happened when I tried," "Day 4, still figuring it out") is more relatable — and
therefore more shareable — than borrowed-guru authority. When a format wants authority
the creator hasn't earned, convert the claim to a process/discovery move and flag any
number that needs verifying. A concept built on a fake number flops the moment the body
fails to show the receipt.

## Useful × entertaining (focused, not niche)

The videos that compound do two jobs at once: they answer a real question for a
**specific** audience (earns saves, follows, trust) *and* entertain the general scroll
that lacks the context (earns reach). Map every concept on both axes:
- **Useful core** — a named group gets a concrete answer to a real question/decision.
- **Entertaining wrapper** — curiosity, surprise, money, conflict, humor anyone enjoys.
Pure niche = no reach. Pure entertainment = no saves and no reason to return. "Focused,
not niche" = precise enough to be saved, broad enough to be enjoyed. Build the useful
core first, then wrap it so a stranger enjoys it without the backstory.

## Ride your winners (series off a hit)

When a video performs, the next video should usually be **its sequel**, not a fresh
idea. A sequel inherits the audience and the algorithm's read of who to show it to —
it starts warm. To build one:
- **Name the series** the hit implies, and frame the new one as Episode 2.
- **Keep the winning DNA** (hook shape, reveal structure, opening framing); change only
  the subject.
- **Open with a callback** ("last time I showed you X — this time…") to pull the
  hit's audience forward.
- **Answer the next question** that audience has after episode 1.
Before brainstorming net-new, always ask: could this be an episode of something already
working?

## Production-constraint-first (the constraint is often the format)

Design the concept around **what can actually be shot**, not an ideal shoot. A limit
usually points at a better, more repeatable format:
- "No interior footage, just a wave + an exterior" → a **top/bottom split-screen**.
- "No time to shoot" → a talking-head + screenshots reveal.
- "Only photos" → a fast photo-montage with overlays and a running counter.
Ask the production reality up front (Stage 0) and let it shape the format. A concept the
creator can shoot today beats a better one they can't.

## Translate facts into the audience's frame

Raw facts don't travel; facts *converted into the audience's world* do. Put numbers in
the units, currency, and comparisons the specific audience lives in, and spell out what
they mean *to them*:
- A China price (¥) → convert to Rupiah for an Indonesian audience, then add the kicker
  ("…and in Indonesia, 2–3× that").
- A metric → tie it to the decision the audience is making.
The conversion *is* the value — it's the line they screenshot and send.

## Success signal: saves & shares, not just views

For useful content, the number that predicts compounding reach is **saves and shares**
(and meaningful comments), not raw views. Saves mean "I need this later" (real utility);
shares mean "this says something for me" (identity/social currency). Design the ending
and caption to earn the specific action you want (see the share/save/comment map above),
and read performance through saves/shares to know what to make next.

## Diagnosing a flat idea (fast checklist)

When an idea feels weak, walk these in order — usually one is the culprit:
0. **No audience / question** — it's made for "everyone." Name the specific group and the
   exact question they're trying to answer. (Most common root cause.)
1. **No cover** — it's a topic, not a concept. Write a 2–5 word in-grid hook.
2. **No number** — find the concrete, exact anchor hiding in the topic.
3. **No open loop** — the title answers itself. Withhold the resolution.
4. **No format** — it's freestyled. Borrow a proven shape from formats.md.
5. **No designed action** — it's "good" but triggers nothing. Pick share/save/comment.
6. **All one emotion** (usually flex) — add a vulnerability/utility beat to balance.
7. **Reinventing instead of sequelling** — a recent hit could carry this as Episode 2.
