# Format archetypes — the proven shapes

Don't freestyle structure. A format is a shape that already travels; you slot real
material into it. Sort an idea by the *job it does for the viewer*, then pick the
shape. When building a Stage-1 menu, deliberately spread across many of these — an
all-one-format menu looks thin and caps out fast.

For each: the shape · the emotional job · the cover-text pattern · the proof/number
it needs · when to reach for it · the failure mode.

---

## 1. Transformation / Before → After
- **Shape:** show the start state, compress time, reveal the result. "Day 1 vs Day X."
- **Job:** proof + aspiration. The viewer projects themselves onto the arc.
- **Cover pattern:** the timeframe + the stakes. "6 MONTH CUT — DAY 1." "30 DAYS, $0 → $X."
- **Needs:** a real before-asset and a real after-number. Without the before, no arc.
- **Reach for it when:** you have a visible change over time (body, revenue, skill,
  a product going from sketch to shipped).
- **Failure mode:** faking or exaggerating the delta. The before must be real.

## 2. Outcome-flex / "I built/made [X] with [one thing]"
- **Shape:** lead with the finished result, then reverse-engineer how. Result-first.
- **Job:** awe + "if they can, maybe I can." FOMO on a method.
- **Cover pattern:** the outcome + the absurd constraint. "$1M BRAND, 2 PEOPLE."
  "ENTIRE AGENCY, 1 AI TOOL."
- **Needs:** a specific, real outcome number. This format lives or dies on the figure.
- **Reach for it when:** you actually produced a concrete result worth opening on.
- **Failure mode:** inflated or unverifiable numbers — instant distrust, and a flop
  when the body can't show the receipts. Honesty-gate hard here.

## 3. Value-dump / "Everything I know about X in 60 seconds"
- **Shape:** rapid, dense enumeration under one umbrella promise, often with a prop
  and a visible timer. Density is the point.
- **Job:** completeness + save-ability — "save this and I've got the whole set."
- **Cover pattern:** scale + time box. "$2M OF ADVICE IN 60 SECONDS." "EVERYTHING I'VE
  LEARNED."
- **Needs:** genuinely useful, non-filler items, and ideally a signature prop.
- **Reach for it when:** you have a lot of real, tactical knowledge on one topic.
- **Failure mode:** thin filler items dressed as a list. Depth dilution.

## 4. Curiosity-gap explainer / "the real reason / the weird thing about X"
- **Shape:** name a tension or a counter-intuitive truth, withhold the resolution
  until the body.
- **Job:** itch that must be scratched. Pure open loop.
- **Cover pattern:** the gap itself. "THE WEIRD SIDE EFFECT." "THE REAL REASON YOU…"
- **Needs:** an actual surprising payoff. The gap must close on something real.
- **Reach for it when:** you know something non-obvious the audience assumes wrong.
- **Failure mode:** clickbait with a weak payoff = bait-and-switch, kills trust.

## 5. Hack / micro-tip / "do this"
- **Shape:** problem → one concrete fix → quick proof. Tight.
- **Job:** instant utility, save-bait.
- **Cover pattern:** the problem or the label. "CUTTING TIP." "STOP DOING THIS."
- **Needs:** one genuinely useful, specific action (and ideally a number — "310 cal,
  30g protein").
- **Reach for it when:** you have a single sharp, replicable trick.
- **Failure mode:** the tip is obvious or everyone already posted it.

## 6. A-vs-B / decision / "X or Y?"
- **Shape:** pose a real either/or the audience debates, then take a side.
- **Job:** decision relief + comment-bait (people argue the other side).
- **Cover pattern:** the two options in tension. "1080 OR 4K?" "iPHONE OR ANDROID FOR X?"
- **Needs:** a genuine, defensible take and a real comparison.
- **Reach for it when:** your audience is stuck on a common fork.
- **Failure mode:** a fake-balanced non-answer. Pick a side.

## 7. Authority-borrow / interview / react / "they said / I say"
- **Shape:** borrow a bigger name's credibility — interview them, react to their clip,
  or stitch/duet a claim.
- **Job:** credibility transfer + tension between two faces/views.
- **Cover pattern:** the big claim + the big name. "$1.3B CEO: YOU ONLY NEED 2 PEOPLE."
- **Needs:** a real source to react to or interview. Two-face tension on the cover.
- **Reach for it when:** you're small but can attach to something big and have a take.
- **Failure mode:** pure reaction with no original point of your own.

## 8. Listicle / numbered inventory
- **Shape:** "5 things…", "7 tools…". Stack comparable items under a quantified promise.
- **Job:** perceived abundance + save the whole set. Add the skipped-number trick
  ("…and the last one nobody expects").
- **Cover pattern:** the count + umbrella. "5 AI TOOLS I ACTUALLY USE."
- **Needs:** items that are each genuinely worth it.
- **Reach for it when:** several loosely-related items, none strong enough alone.
- **Failure mode:** filler to hit the count.

## 9. Street-interview / voyeur ("how much do you make?")
- **Shape:** approach strangers, ask the question everyone's curious about, label them.
- **Job:** social voyeurism — the answers you're "not supposed" to ask.
- **Cover pattern:** the spicy question or the subject label. "FINANCIAL FLEX." "TIPPER."
- **Needs:** real subjects and a question with built-in tension.
- **Reach for it when:** the curiosity is *about other people*, not about you.
- **Failure mode:** staged/fake answers — audiences smell it.

## 10. Reaction / rant / pattern-interrupt
- **Shape:** strong emotional reaction to a thing — annoyance, disbelief, delight.
- **Job:** recognition + catharsis ("finally someone said it").
- **Cover/opening pattern:** the relatable grievance. "STOP PUTTING ME ON DISPLAY PHONES."
- **Needs:** a genuinely shared annoyance or surprise; high-energy delivery.
- **Reach for it when:** something in your world is universally irritating or absurd.
- **Failure mode:** ranting about something only you care about.

## 11. Day-in-the-build / build-in-public episode (named series)
- **Shape:** a real slice of building something ambitious, framed as an episode.
- **Job:** parasocial belonging + ambition. The viewer joins a quest.
- **Cover pattern:** episode + an episode-specific hook, NOT just "Day 4." "DAY 4: I LET
  MY AGENT SPEND MY MONEY."
- **Needs:** a real concrete thing happening that day (a task, a win, a failure).
- **Reach for it when:** you're building in public and want a returning audience.
- **Failure mode:** "Day N" with no hook of its own — the series name isn't a hook.

## 12. Contrarian / myth-bust
- **Shape:** "everyone tells you X — that's wrong, here's why." Take down a common belief.
- **Job:** insider correction + identity ("now I'm in the know").
- **Cover pattern:** the myth, negated. "YOU'RE USING AI WRONG." "PROTEIN TIMING IS A MYTH."
- **Needs:** a real, defensible counter-position (opinion is fine if owned honestly).
- **Reach for it when:** you genuinely disagree with consensus and can defend it.
- **Failure mode:** contrarian for its own sake, with no substance behind it.

## 13. POV / relatability / "the things we…"
- **Shape:** name a small universal truth or behavior; let the viewer feel seen.
- **Job:** recognition, shareable because "this is so me / send to a friend."
- **Cover pattern:** the half-said observation. "THE THINGS WE PAY ATTENTION TO."
- **Needs:** a real, specific, slightly under-said human truth.
- **Reach for it when:** the value is a *feeling of being seen*, not utility.
- **Failure mode:** too generic — "Mondays be like." Specific beats broad.

## 14. Challenge / stunt
- **Shape:** attempt something with a clear constraint or stake; show the attempt.
- **Job:** vicarious thrill + "will it work?" open loop.
- **Cover pattern:** the dare. "TRYING THE £20 UNLIMITED STEAK." "$1 vs $100 X."
- **Needs:** a real attempt with a genuine outcome (success or funny failure).
- **Reach for it when:** you can actually do the stunt and it's visual.
- **Failure mode:** low-stakes stunt nobody cares about the result of.

---

## How formats combine

The strongest concepts wrap **utility inside interiority** — a number/tip inside a
feeling/curiosity. Common power-combos:
- Transformation + Outcome-flex — the arc *and* the receipt.
- Day-in-the-build + Contrarian — an episode whose hook is a hot take.
- Value-dump + Listicle — counted, dense, max save-ability.
- Authority-borrow + Reaction — attach to a big claim and add your take.

**Series framing bolts onto any format.** Turn any concept into "episode N of [named
series]" and you convert one-off views into a returning audience — but only if each
episode carries its own hook on the cover.
