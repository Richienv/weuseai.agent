# Creative short-form video ideas — the audience-first packaging engine

Most "video ideas" fail for one of two reasons: nobody decided *who it's for and
what it answers*, or it was sold as a *topic* instead of a *packaged concept*. This
skill fixes both, in that order. It does **not** write the script (that's the
`script-writer` skill). It decides *who we're making this for, what question it
answers, what the video is, and why anyone stops scrolling.*

## Two principles, in order

**1. Start with the audience and the question — not the topic.** Before any
packaging, name the specific person this is for and the specific question or
decision they're trying to resolve. Content that answers a real question for a real
group gets *saved and shared* (the signals that actually drive reach); content
made for "everyone" lands on no one. A topic is "my classmates' cars." An
audience+question is "Indonesian parents deciding whether China is worth it, asking
*what kind of people will my kid be around?*" — same footage, completely different
video, and only the second one travels.

**2. An idea is not a topic — it's a topic wearing a package.** Once you know the
who and the why, the package is what sells it in the half-second a thumb hovers:
cover text, first frame, a number, a curiosity gap, a proven format. Never hand
back a topic. Hand back a concept already 80% sold before a word is spoken.

The failure mode both guard against: earnest, accurate content ("a video showing
how my agent triages email") that serves no one in particular and has no
scroll-stopping surface — smart, true, and invisible. The fix is always: pick the
person, answer their question, wrap it in a package proven to travel.

## The "useful to one, entertaining to all" target

The strongest concepts are **focused, not niche.** They answer a real question for a
specific group precisely enough to earn saves *and* are entertaining enough for the
general scroll to enjoy without needing the context. Aim for both:
- **Useful core** → a specific group gets a real answer → saves, follows, trust.
- **Entertaining wrapper** → curiosity, surprise, money, conflict, humor → reach.
A "tuition costs in China" video saved by parents *and* enjoyed by everyone who
likes a money-reveal is the target. Pure niche = no reach; pure entertainment = no
saves and no reason to come back. Build the useful core, then wrap it.

## What "the game" is (the levers that make things travel)

A concept travels when it has **at least two** of these firing at once:

1. **A cover that is a second hook.** 2–5 bold words *on the frame itself*. The cover
   sells in the grid; the spoken hook sells once they're in. No cover plan = unfinished.
2. **A specific number** — and the more *exact*, the more it reads as true.
   "Rp 798,773,278" outperforms "around 800 million." Numbers are concrete,
   screenshot-able, argue-able.
3. **A curiosity gap stated and withheld** — the title opens a loop it refuses to close.
4. **A proven format (don't freestyle structure)** — see `references/formats.md`.
5. **A reason to act — share, save, or comment** (pick one, design for it) — see
   `references/mechanics.md`.
6. **A first frame worth freezing on** — a face mid-emotion, the result, the before, a prop.

Strong concepts wrap a *number/utility* inside a *feeling/curiosity*. A naked tip is
forgettable; a naked feeling is unactionable.

## Ride your winners (turn a hit into a series)

When a video works, the highest-leverage next move is **not** a fresh idea — it's
**Episode 2 of the one that hit.** A sequel inherits the audience *and* the
algorithm's understanding of who to show it to, so it starts warm instead of cold.
When the user has a video that performed, before brainstorming net-new:
- **Name the series** the hit implies ("what studying in China is actually like").
- **Keep the winning DNA** — same hook shape, same number-reveal structure, same
  opening location/framing — and change only the subject.
- **Open by referencing the hit** ("last time I showed you X — this time…") to pull
  its audience forward.
- **Each episode answers the next question** that audience has after the last one.
Always ask whether a new idea could instead be an episode of something already working.

## The workflow — two stages

Default to **menu first, then deepen** unless the user clearly wants only one.

### Stage 0 — Intake (ask if missing, via multiple-choice)

Lock these before generating. If anything's unclear, ask via the **AskUserQuestion
tool**, never open prose — picking is faster than writing. The first two are the
spine; don't skip them to get to packaging faster.

1. **AUDIENCE** — who *specifically* is this for? Not "everyone." A group with a
   shared situation, question, or identity. Lead with *their* feeling and need.
2. **THE QUESTION / DECISION** — what is that audience actually trying to figure out
   or decide? The video's job is to answer it concretely. (This is the "search
   intent" behind the scroll — it's what earns the save.)
3. **RAW MATERIAL** — the real thing to mine: a result, a number, a screenshot, a
   story, a process, an opinion, a failure. The moat is *specific lived material*.
   If you're inventing the substance, stop and ask for it.
4. **CREATOR / VOICE** — whose account and through-line? Use any creator profile in
   context (voice, story, honesty standard) to sharpen and to power the honesty gate.
5. **GOAL + PROVEN WINNERS** — reach / trust / saves / conversion? And: *has anything
   already worked that this could be a sequel to?* (See "Ride your winners.")
6. **PLATFORM + PRODUCTION REALITY** — Reels/Shorts/TikTok, and **what can actually be
   shot?** Design the format around the available footage, not an ideal shoot — a
   constraint ("no interior footage, only a wave + an exterior") often *is* the format
   (e.g. a top/bottom split). See `references/mechanics.md`.

State them back in one line ("Audience X · Question Y · Material Z · Goal/winner W ·
Platform/shootable V") and proceed.

### Stage 1 — The menu (volume, scannable)

Generate **15–20** packaged concepts as a scannable menu — volume to beat
idea-scarcity. Each line is ONE line, already packaged, and tied to the audience +
question from Stage 0:

```
[#] TITLE / spoken hook — cover: "COVER TEXT" · format: <archetype> · number/proof: <x> · answers: <the question> · travels on: <lever> · do: <share|save|comment>
```

Rules: spread across formats and across the emotional map (not all flex); every line
has a cover and (where possible) an exact number; flag honesty risks `⚠ confirm real`;
star your top 3–5 with a one-clause reason. Then ask which to deepen.

### Stage 2 — Deepen the chosen ones

Expand each picked concept into a full package for `script-writer`:

```
## [Working title]
- Audience + question it answers: <who · what they're deciding>
- Spoken hook (first 2s): "<line said out loud>"
- Cover text (in-grid 2nd hook): "<2–5 words>"
- First frame: <face/result/before/prop>
- Format: <archetype + 1-line skeleton from formats.md>
- The number / proof: <exact, real anchor + what asset shows it>
- One feeling: <pride / fear / recognition / relief / outrage / awe>
- Travels on: <lever> → intended action: <share | save | comment>
- Series / binge hook: <if a series: name + episode-specific hook; if a sequel, the callback>
- Honesty check: <PASS, or what to convert/confirm>
- → Next: hand to script-writer for the per-shot script.
```

Then offer to run `script-writer`.

## Delivery order & formatting (how to lay out the final script doc)

Lead with what the creator shoots; bury the reasoning. And keep it clean — assume the
creator is fluent. Signal each element with **formatting, not labels**: don't write
"Spoken hook:", "Cover text:", "Audio:", "Delivery:" — the creator knows what things
are, and the labels just slow the read. The doc should be ~98% content; cut filler and
keep placeholders to the few things the creator must actually fill.

Order, top to bottom:
1. **Title** as the H1 (it doubles as the primary on-screen text), with a few alt titles
   as a short italic list.
2. **The script**, per-shot, using one consistent typographic convention — e.g.:
   - **bold shot number** · *italic = camera / visual direction*
   - plain text in quotes = what they say (audio)
   - **bold** = on-screen text/caption · *running total in italics*
   - *italic after an em-dash* = delivery note
   The spoken hook is just shot 1's line — no separate "hook" block.
3. **A divider**, then everything else at the bottom, tightened: strategy (audience,
   question, conflict, format, self-score), layout, capture, number tables, honesty,
   confirms, parked ideas — reference material, terse and formatting-led.

Never open with strategy. Never use label prefixes in the script body. Phrase the few
needed placeholders so they don't break the read (prefer "temen gw" over
"[SWAP friend name here]").

## The honesty gate (keep it even in the general engine)

Never fabricate a number, result, credential, or follower count. When a format wants
authority the creator hasn't earned, convert the claim to **discovery/process** voice
("here's what happened when I tried") — also a *better* hook for a small account.
Flag every unverified number so the user can confirm or swap it.

## How this relates to script-writer

Concept → craft. This skill decides audience, question, and package; `script-writer`
decides how it's said shot-by-shot. Always pass a deepened concept (with its audience
+ question attached) to `script-writer` rather than scripting here.

## Quick filter before you ship a concept (5-axis idea score)

Score 1–10, name the weakest, fix it:
- **Scroll-stop** — cover + first frame stop the thumb in the grid?
- **Curiosity** — an open loop I *need* closed?
- **Specificity** — a real, exact number/detail (not a vibe)?
- **Shareability/save** — a clear reason to share, save, or comment, tied to the audience?
- **Payoff** — does the body deliver what the cover promised? (No bait-and-switch.)

Under ~30/50 = a topic in a costume. Re-package or drop it.
