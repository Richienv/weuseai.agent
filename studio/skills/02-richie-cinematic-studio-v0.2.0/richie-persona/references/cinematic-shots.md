# Cinematic shots — make a MOVIE SCENE, not a photo

Richie's bar: every shot should look like a **Hollywood film still**, not an AI picture. This is what
gets it there (learned the hard way — heavy B&W looked cheap; this looks like cinema):

## The premium standard (apply to every shot)
```
Hyper-realistic film still, shot on ARRI Alexa, anamorphic lens, shallow depth of field,
fine 35mm film grain, rich cinematic color grade (deep teal-blue shadows + subtle warm key),
heavy atmospheric haze/mist, motivated light only, photoreal skin with pores and a faint sweat sheen.
A movie scene, not a flat photo.
```
- Avoid the cheap look: no heavy/flat black-and-white, no over-desaturation, no studio-clean.

## The shadow trick (hides any likeness gap + reads as cinema)
Put **half the face into deep shadow** — low-key chiaroscuro, a single hard key, the far half falling
off into black. It looks like real film lighting AND quietly hides the ~10–15% where a generated face
isn't a perfect match. Use this whenever exact likeness is at risk (close-ups, hero shots).

## Consistency across a set
"Same camera, only different lens." Keep the **grade, grain, color, and mist identical** across all
shots of a sequence; change only the **lens/framing** (wide / medium / close). In two-character facing
shots, keep the **distance and scale matched** both directions.

## Naturalism = the ANGLE only
"Natural" means the camera angle (off-centre, rule of thirds, slight handheld imperfection) — NOT lower
production value. Keep it premium and sharp.

## Emotion
Exhausted/tired reads best when he's **still handsome and composed** — heavy eyelids, worn-out stare,
faint sweat — not gritty or over-acted. Younger self can be dead-serious and intense.
